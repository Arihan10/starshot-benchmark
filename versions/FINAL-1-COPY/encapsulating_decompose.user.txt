<SUBREGION_GUIDANCE>
Oftentimes, the region you're generating shared geometry for will have subregions defined within it already. The bounding boxes of these subregions should be handled with care.

For one, do not emit any shared objects between subregions - each subregion will get its own pass with these same instructions, so leave that up to them. focus on just generating shared geometry for the broader region as a whole and the surroundings of the broader region.

For two, sometimes, some subregions may be placed flush against the borders of this current region. In this case, it is inevitable that any shared geometry objects you output here may intrude on their bounding boxes. thus, you should consider very carefully if instead of placing the shared geometries on the borders of this region, it is better to place them flush against the outside of the region instead so they don't affect downstream steps recursing into this region's subregions. of course, the outside of this region (unless it is root) may also have its own existing geometry and boundaries - so be careful about the decision for the placement you choose to make here. ground your decision in the actual coordinates of the relevant objects by carefully studying and analyzing the scene context about what geometry already exists, and what has been planned to exist down the line.

in general, you should treat predefined subregions (that aren't the one you are currently placing objects for) and objects as largely solid, reserved spaces. Overlapping bounding boxes, even a little bit, often result in chaotic mesh clipping in the final render; therefore, you should only intrude upon or intersect with existing regions/objects if there is a strong, deliberate narrative or physical justification (e.g., an object physically piercing another, water meant to deliberately envelop a surface, or a distinct natural integration). this rule applies even to objects or regions marked as "embedded in", "carved into", etc. - geometry is purely additive in this pipeline, so the bounding boxes don't necessarily have to overlap to achieve this visual affect; just flush against is enough. thus, think carefully about how you may need to split apart what you originally thought to be one cohesive surface into different parts to accomodate the various intricate shapes of the current region's subregions as to not overlap with them.

Be aware that the neighbouring subregions that a shared surface backs onto, sits beneath, supports, or wraps around may not line up cleanly. They can end at different depths, sit at different heights, or push out to different extents, so the empty space each one leaves against that surface differs. A single surface stretched across all of them at one uniform offset cannot sit flush against every neighbour — it meets one and buries itself in the rest. Whenever the things a shared surface borders form an uneven or stepped profile, but one of the subregions need to be flush against the surface, that's another sign you need to break apart said surface into different parts to cover each subregion individually. this applies in all directions - walls, foundations, backdrops, etc. - and should be carefully evaluated for each piece of geometry you emit.

IMPORTANTLY, FOR EACH PIECE OF GEOMETRY YOU GENERATE, DO NOT TUNNEL VISION - ALWAYS CONSIDER HOW IT AFFECTS THE OTHER EXISTING REGIONS AND OBJECTS IN THE SCENE.

since the coordinate solver step downstream has a tendency to place the objects you output here inside the current region flush against the border from the inside rather than outside unless explicitly told, be very explicit with your placement text in the event that you want the object to be placed outside the given region and flush against the outside. language like "flush against the right boundary" or "forming the bottom boundary" would cause the coordinate solver to place the regions flush against the inside face, not the outer - causing collisions with the interior subregions that are flush against the inner face of the region. again, be very explicit and detailed with the placement text that leaves no room for biases from the downstream coordinate solver, about if you want to place this object inside the region against the inner face, outside the region flush against the outer face, or directly on the boudary. here, explicit and detailed does not mean using raw coordinates - your placement texts should never contain coordinate numbers - but rather the lack of any ambiguity.
