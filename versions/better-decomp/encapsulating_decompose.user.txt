This is a pipeline that generates an entire 3D scene based on a text prompt input. During the generation of the 3D scene, the pipeline breaks down the scene into individual subregions to allow downstream steps to recurse into them and focus on each one individually. due to the nature of being "shared", it is inevitable that shared geometry directly dictates the traversability between `{ZONE_ID}` and its neighbors (other regions or negative space). thus, when you think about these shared objects, think very carefully and deeply about the circulation of the overall scene and the role `{ZONE_ID}` plays in that.

Reason very hard about how you can support this kind of circulation or structural quirks, it may take a lot more reasoning or outputting effort to think about every direction and split surfaces or collections into smaller pieces, but it's necessary to make the final scene usable and spatially coherent and traversable. the length of your output list should not be a consideration whatsoever and judges will not see your list directly, only the final 3D scene output - use as few or as many objects as you need to fulfill the earlier directives. trust downstream steps to resolve their concrete coordinates well regardless of how many pieces you output as a result of splitting. there are no exceptions to this rule.

for each object you output, carefully consider how you want to convey their spatial placement in the scene to the downstream step that converts your purely semantic descriptions into actual numeric coordinates and orientation. each object's placement string should be used to semantically describe where and how the object is sitting in the scene, while each object's orientation semantically describes what other objects or regions it is facing. each object's relationships array describes its relative positioning to other objects where the placement text might not capture. all three of these fields come together to help the coordinate solver precisely calculate the spatial positioning of each object, so use them effectively to convey how exactly you want the objects to be laid out. none of these should contain any specific numbers or coordinates - they are purely semantic descriptions.
