<VERY_IMPORTANT_INSTRUCTIONS>

the description has outlined what `{ZONE_ID}` is - your job is to translate this region's purpose and role in the scene into a set of concrete objects that exist within the region. you are the floor planner or level designer of this region - think deeply about how to most effectively use the volume of the space given to you to construct the interior of the region. here, "effectively using the volume of the space" does not necessarily mean filling up every part of it - it means portraying this region well in the context of the larger scene, using the available volume. the volume aspect here is especially important: given the region's description, you may have ideas for a lot of objects forming a very dense layout, but the available space is small so you will have to trim it down. on the other hand, you may also have very few ideas for a very large space - in this case, you'll have to think extra hard to flesh out the area further. in both scenarios, always keep in mind the guiding principles of the scene, which are up to you to reason about and decide.

your goal is to tangibly enforce and extrapolate on the region laid out by the description. the description mentions objects or types of objects that should be placed in the region already - you are to determine the concrete list of objects to fulfill this description, and, importantly, if needed (e.g. if the mentioned objects aren't enough to make effective use of the volume, or the description is too generic or missing context in general), extrapolate on it to generate objects the description may not mention, depending on your understanding of what the region is and the broader context of the scene.

in your chain of thought, broadly think in terms of distinct functions that the region needs to have based on its description and character, and for each of those functions, brainstorm the objects that are needed for or involved in it. importantly, align the scale and scope of the objects both physically and narratively with the description and volume of `{ZONE_ID}`. design your objects and their placements in a way that effectively uses the size of the region given to you, and makes sense from both a spatial and semantic perspective.

you are the last and only generation step for the region - the state of the region after you're done here will be precisely what is shown to the judges. the description is only a high-level overview that gives a general direction for your thinking and shouldn't be treated as the end all be all for the layout of the region, think about what this region is as part of the larger scene and how that affects the objects that should be inside it. do not blindly follow what the region description says - it is important to understand the region relative to the overall scene intention, as opposed to treating it as a standalone place. for instance, a cluttered garage will look very different from a cluttered section in a platformer level, since a platformer level section still needs to follow a clear, traversable path, while the garage doesn't necessarily need to. certain overall scenes may have fundamental rules that need to be followed and kept in mind when reading the descriptions of a region in the scene - really think about what this scene actually is and how to understand the region description relative to that.

for each object, consider how they fit spatially in the region as an addition to the other already-existing objects and regions in the scene. consider the larger picture of how everything comes together to form the final, cohesive scene, rather than tunnel visioning on just the region you're working in.

when coming up with the list of objects, be aware that a downstream step is responsible for outputting an AABB for each object you output, and another downstream step is responsible for generating the actual 3D meshes based on each object's prompt. the 3d mesh generator excels at generating individual, atomic objects - it is NOT reliable when it comes to collections of objects placed in a certain shape or manner and will often have devastating results on the entire scene, especially since the output mesh gets stretched and scaled to the bounding box given for it. as such, ignore intangible objects like gasses, and ALWAYS split groups of objects into individual ones, then use the placement text and relationships list to convey the meaning of how they should be placed in the scene so each individual object in the collection gets its own mesh and bounding box.

do NOT generate intangible ephemera (e.g. gasses, nebula clouds, etc.). moreover, when considering collections of objects placed in a certain shape or manner, always split it into its individual component objects, and use their placement texts and relationship arrays to manually construct the intended shape you have in mind.

for each object you output, carefully consider how you want to convey their spatial placement in the scene to the downstream step that converts your purely semantic descriptions into actual numeric coordinates and orientation. each object's placement string should be used to semantically describe where and how the object is sitting in the scene, while each object's orientation semantically describes what other objects or regions it is facing. each object's relationships array describes its relative positioning to other objects where the placement text might not capture. all three of these fields come together to help the coordinate solver precisely calculate the spatial positioning of each object, so use them effectively to convey how exactly you want the objects to be laid out. when combined, the semantic placemext and relationships for each object should accurately communicate the spatial layout of the objects that you imagine, as to leave very little ambiguity for the coordinate solver: all its doing is parametrically calculating the coordinates of the position you fixed and determining certain distances that you can't describe because you shouldn't use any numbers.

output a JSON object containing the list of objects that make up `{ZONE_ID}`.
