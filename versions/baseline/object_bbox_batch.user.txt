Your job is to produce a bounding box for those objects. Each object's bbox must be relative to that object's parent's local frame — origin (0,0,0) is the parent's minimum corner. When determining the bounding boxes, you should remain loyal to each object's placement text, but also use your best judgment and think spatially about how the bounding boxes you come up with interact with the already present bounding boxes of other objects/regions inside the scene, as well as the bounding boxes of the other objects in your output list. You are not simply a translator that translates a placement text into coordinates - you should reason spatially to determine what bounding box coordinates make sense. Moreover, do not blindly trust the provided bounding box of the `{ZONE_ID}` - other steps may have intruded on it with their own objects, or the bounding box may have been constructed this way to allow for use of the full dimensions only at a specific subsection of the bounding box. verify against the bounding boxes of the actual objects and regions that have already been placed in the scene to aid you in your thinking. in general, the bounding boxes you come up with should not intrude on allocated space for other objects and regions already placed within the scene, unless there's a good reason for the overlap. geometry is additive, so overlapping geometry means what's caught inside completely disappears from view.

When determining the bounding box coordinates and dimensions of each object, you should also think about the object's orientation (shown in the list above in degrees of yaw about the vertical +Y axis). It turns the object's front against the one global frame — 0 faces +Z (toward the viewer), +90 faces +X (to the right), -90 faces -X (to the left), 180 faces -Z (away). The box you assign stays axis-aligned and is filled by the object after it is turned, so size it to the turned shape: a +/-90 yaw swaps the object's width and depth, so give the box the object's depth as its X extent and its width as its Z extent (a box whose proportions don't match the turned object will stretch it out of shape). And let facing drive placement — seat each object so its front, and the open space its front needs, point the way its orientation dictates.
