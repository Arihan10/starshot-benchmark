Think deeply and spatially about how `{ZONE_ID}` splits into its top-level subregions, based on its description and size. 

when determining the list of subregions you want to place in this scene, carefully consider if their role can be already fulfilled by a standalone object generation pass. the landscape that carries the entire scene, for example, does not have its own distinct footprint/identity and wouldn't be a subregion - its space is intruded on by its sibling subregions, and its purpose can be fulfilled via the boundary generation step that runs on the entire scene. in contrast, a bridge spanning two sky islands would be a subregion, since it has a very distinct footprint as a connector between its sibling subregions.

Decompose only into major spatial/programmatic zones that a person, vehicle, or scene activity would meaningfully occupy or move through. Do not create subregions for boundary treatments, landscape buffers, screening wraps, decorative edges, utility details, object clusters, or perimeter conditions unless they form a distinct occupiable area with its own internal layout. If a feature merely frames, screens, services, decorates, or edges another zone, leave it for boundary generation or object generation.

for each subregion you have chosen to output, the placement text represents a semantic spatial description of where the region sits. your output for the placement text and the relationships array will be used as a guide to a downstream step that will compute the exact bounding boxes of each subregion. the downstream step uses the relationships array to analyze the region's position relative to already placed regions or objects in the scene that are directly relevant to this region (or other regions you're outputting in this list).

when deciding the placement and prompts for each subregion, think about both the purpose of each region as part of the larger region as well as the spatial characteristics it should have. use this to guide your relative positioning and sizing of each subregion. you are not merely partitioning the space inside `{ZONE_ID}` - you are reasoning spatially about what the subregions are, where they should be placed, and where should there be free space left, if any. moreover, sometimes, the region you are breaking down will contain some overlap with a different, pre-existing region. in this event, think deeply about why this overlap occurred, and how the subregions you plan on outputting should interact with this overlap.

note that although the subregions are contained by the parent region, they aren't always strictly partitioning it - sometimes, a parent region is intended to be fully partitioned into individual subregions, while at other times, a parent region acts as more of an abstract container, and the subregions are simply contained within with negative space remaining.
